GDD
Gameplay: 
Darkest Dungeon inspired. Start with just Ceci, but then collect marionettes throughout a run to add to the party. Make your way through battles to get to the boss


Aesthetics:
* Dark, desolate, similar to Darkest Dungeon
* Spacious, lots of signs that there used to be people here, but no one is left
* Lonely, isolating


Marionettes:
Marionettes can be one of several classes. Each class has a lot of skills, but a marionette will spawn with 3-4 skills selected from the pool of skills that the class has, 1 random perfection and 1 random imperfection. When a marionette’s HP is depleted, it is destroyed. Marionettes can gain Perfections, and Imperfections from events and sometimes dealing and receiving crits. Perfections can increase various stats, while Imperfections reduce various stats. Marionettes have levels, reaching higher levels increases the Marionette’s stats. There is a global maximum level, Marionettes cannot level up past the level. Each class has a unique base stat and stat growth. Clicking on a Marionette opens the stat screen.  




Ceci:
Ceci has lots of skills. Players can pick 4 to bring before starting the run. Ceci's skills are very varied, allowing the player to put her into any role by picking the corresponding skills. When Ceci’s HP is depleted, the run ends.


Enemies:
Enemies are based on the Justice fan mascots, mutated and deformed into monstrous versions of themselves.


Elites:
* Elites are based on Advent fan mascots, mutated and deformed into monstrous versions. They are essentially minibosses with simple mechanics. Elites spawn with normal enemies as well. Elites generally have higher stats than normal enemies, especially health and damage.
* Jailbird: A large crow. The mechanic is “Use Fly, gaining dodge buff and must be hit 3 times in a turn to ground again -> if not grounded, use Terror Song, dealing massive AOE damage and stun all player team”
* Stats: high dodge
* If successfully knocked to the ground, will spend the next turn using a weak skill Peck
* Novellite: Eldritch monster. Mechanic is “Use Eldritch Grasp, applying damage receive increased debuff on character with lowest current health and move the target to the front row -> Use Devour on the front row character, dealing massive damage”
* Pebble: A huge rock golem, spawns alone. Mechanic is “when this enemy dies, split into 2 enemies, and then split into 2 enemies again.” Pebble can split into a total of 4 smaller pebbles
* Stats: high protection
* Attacks have high stun chance
* Ruffian: 2 monstrous dogs. Mechanic is “when one dies, but the other is alive, revive next turn at 30% health. When one attacks, the other also attacks the same target.”
* Attacks have high bleed chance
Bosses:
Before getting all the conditions for the true ending, a run will end with either of the 2 following bosses:
* Flaming Rose-Knight Marionette:
* Greatsword wielder with telegraphed attacks that deal huge damage and apply burn DOT(telegraphs are similar to Prophet in Darkest Dungeon)
* Can summon minions and buff them with a status effect that increases their damage dealt, but also DOT them
* AOE that doesn’t deal much damage but apply a unique burn DOT
* God-Eye Panther Marionette:
* Rely more on minions than direct attacks(similar to Necromancer in Darkest Dungeon)
* Can apply debuff that makes all attacks received crit
* Can apply self buff that dodges all attacks
* Can apply buff to a minion to make all attacks guaranteed to hit and ignore protection
* Can apply Marked status
When one boss spawns for a run, its spawn chance for the next run is reduced by 10%.
Marionette Classes:
* Maid: Support, healer
* Butler: Tank, protect teammates
* Knight: Damage dealer
* Princess: Support, buffer, force allies to protect self
* World is Mine: Low damage skill that gives 1 part if it kills an enemy, once per battle.
* Minstrel(plays violin): Buffs allies
* Sharpshooter: Backrow target, buff allies damage
* Assassin: Setup burst damage, crit focused
* Enforcer: Tank damage hybrid, debuffs enemies
* Commander: Tank Support hybrid, buffs allies
* Alchemist: DOT, healer


Final Boss Fight
3 phases boss fight. Nevergreen has 2 actions per turn. Phase 1 lasts until the boss reaches 60% health. Phase 2 lasts until the end of the HP bar. Phase 3 has a separate HP bar. During phase 1, Nevergreen uses powered up versions of skills Ceci has access to, while occasionally summoning marionettes. During phase 2, she does the same, but can also steal player’s marionettes with her puppeteer strings(limited to 1 at a time). Stolen marionettes take damage each turn until the player destroys the puppeteer strings by attacking it. Phase 3, Nevergreen no longer summon marionettes or steal from the player, but Chained-Spirit Chaser Marionette joins the battle. Chained-Spirit Chaser has 2 actions per turn, hits hard, has an attack that moves the target all the way back, an attack that moves the target all the way forward, and an uppercut that stuns a target.
Story:
The story unveils as the player progresses through the run. Story is revealed through lore text of various items and marionettes, game events, as well as dialogues and monologues.
Main Theme:
The story mainly deals with the following themes: being “alive”, memories of loved ones.


Background Lore:
The state of the world is post-apocalyptic, caused by Hololive Advents after they escaped. Justice failed to capture them, allowing them to bring about a catastrophe known only as Advent of The Five Suns. All the enemies are people who once worked for the organization, but transformed into twisted versions of the mascots of Justice, Advent’s greatest enemies, as petty vengeance for chasing them down. The current whereabouts of the Justice members are unknown. Advent have attained unimaginable power and ascended becoming the new suns.


Maybe Justice and Advent was on good terms before. Smth happened that led Advent to turn down this extreme path. They spare Justice, only take them into custody because they still have lingering affection for Justice.
Plot:
Ceci wakes up and finds herself in a dilapidated building(this is Justice’s HQ). With no memory of where she is, and why she’s here, she sets out to explore the place. She stumbles on some enemies crowding around something. They attack her. She beats them and checks out what they were crowding around. It’s a broken automaton that looks just like Ceci. Somehow, by instinct, she extends puppeteer wires from her fingers towards the automaton, reanimating it into a marionette.


Ceci continues to look around. She notices many broken automatons heading in the same direction. Without a second thought, she heads towards the same destination too. Throughout Ceci’s exploration of the location, she’ll run into various events that reveal more about her past and lead Ceci to questioning what she is. 


At the destination of her journey, Ceci finds a room of piled up destroyed automatons. At the top of the pile sits an automaton, looking just like Ceci again, but slightly different. Her color appears faded, yet she is much less damaged than all other automatons Ceci has come across. The automaton opens her eyes and leers at Ceci, “What you are looking for isn’t here. Nothing is here anymore. But fret not, Cecilia. I shall lay you to rest. Your worries, your struggles, confide them all to me. So that I can make you forget it all, like I have done for countless other Cecilias.” The 2 enters battle, and the automaton’s name is revealed to be Cecilia Nevergreen.


During phase 2 transition, Nevergreen asks, “why must you fight back? Do you not want to rest?” Ceci answers, “there is someone I must find no matter what.” Nevergreen retorts, “All the other Cecilias who made it here said the same thing. You are no different. You, just like all of us, are an automaton without purpose. Lay down your arms now. Let me put you to rest.” Ceci refuses, “my purpose is for me to decide. I will live how I see fit.”


At phase 3 transition, Nevergreen is desperate, “very well, if you want to find her so much.” Nevergreen flicks her wrist, and a familiar gauntlet is lowered from above with puppeteer strings. It looks old and damaged. Ceci is shocked, “that gauntlet…” A marionette rises from among the pile of automatons. But this one doesn’t look like Cecilia, this one looks like Gigi. Nevergreen explains, “Long, long ago, I was just like all of you. All I wanted was to see her again. I searched and searched, but she was nowhere to be found. So I built her. But this isn’t her. This is an automaton, just like us. A simulacrum of a living being. No matter how similar she looks, how similar she acts, she, we are not alive. Do you see the futility of your charade now, Cecilia?” Nevergreen puts the gauntlet on the Gigi automaton, and she joins the battle as Chained-Spirit Chaser Marionette.


After defeating the final boss, Ceci asks her, “do you know where Gigi is?” Nevergreen answers, “I do not.” Ceci asks, “Do you know if she’s still alive?” Nevergreen answers, “I do not.” Ceci remains silent. Nevergreen asks, “will you spend the rest of your life looking for her?” Ceci answers, “I don’t know… But I know that whether I find her or not, I won’t give up on living.”

Gameloop Flow:
1. Before starting a run, the player can decide which 4 skills to give to CC. Once the player finishes choosing skills, they can start a run.
  

2. The player enters a room and starts a battle. Once the player finishes the battle, they can choose 1 of 3 different routes. Each route will have a label showing what reward the player will get in that room. The very first room of a run will always reward a marionette.
  

  

3. Once a room is chosen, the player moves to that room, depending on the reward type, there might or might not be a battle in that room. The gameplay loop then repeats until the player has moved through enough rooms to reach the boss fight.
4. The boss room only has 1 route to proceed. If the player has not met the criteria for the true boss fight, the screen fades to black once she enters, shows some run statistics, and brings the player back to pre-run preparations. If the player has met the criteria for the true boss fight, then the final boss cutscene starts.
Stats:
* Ceci, Marionettes, and enemies all have the following stats.
* Attack: Damage output of character. A character’s attack is a range, between 80%-120% of a central value, rounded to an integer.
* Defense(%): damage taken is reduced by this percent
* Accuracy(%): character’s base accuracy, determine likelihood of hitting enemies with a skill. Attacks are all capped at 95% accuracy.
* Dodge(%): character’s dodge chance, determine likelihood of avoiding an attack, subtracted from an attack’s accuracy
* Critical Change(%): determine likelihood of landing a critical hit, increasing damage by x1.5
* Speed: Used to determine turn order.
* Stats can be further modified by buffs and debuffs
* Skills can scale with stats differently, for example, 120% of attack stat, or -60% critical chance.
* Some skills can ignore certain stats, such as ignoring defense, or ignoring dodge.


Resistances:
* Characters can have resistances to various status effects.
* Resistances are percentage based, and is subtracted from a source’s status effect application chance
* Example, character A has 20% poison resistance, character B targets A with a skill that has 90% poison chance, the final chance to poison character A is 90% - 20% = 70%


Statuses:
* Statuses include buffs and debuffs
* Buffs can alter various stats, provides constant heals, add special effects to characters
* Debuffs can alter various stats, provides constant damage, add special effects to characters
* Statuses all have an amplitude, a duration, and an effect
* Status are tracked and expire individually, but displayed as a stack, and behave as if they are applied to calculations simultaneously. Example: 3 stack of bleed expire individually, but they deal damage one equal to the number of stacks.
* Status List:
* Move: moves a character in a direction, amplitude = spaces moved, duration = 0
* Bleed: deals damage equal to amplitude at the start of a character’s turn for duration number of turns, attacks that cause bleed deal more damage than attacks that apply blight, but bleed tends to have lower amplitude than blight
* Blight: same as bleed, but different resistance, attacks that apply blight tends to deal little damage, but blight tends to have higher amplitude than bleed
* Stun: makes a character skips their turn, amplitude = 0, duration = 1. Once stun wears off, character gains stun resistance +300%
* Debuff: various effects, amplitude, and duration, usually reduce character stat, but can also hold misc effects
* Mark: amplitude: 0, duration = turns before it wears off. Makes all enemies attack the character, if applied on the player team. Increase damage of some attack when attacking a marked target.
* Buff: increases specified stats equal to amplitude for duration of turns
* Guard: When applied to an ally, the character takes damage instead of the ally when the ally attacks. Guard is immediately broken when the guardian is stunned, when the guard is guarded by another character, the guardian receives guard,  or when the guardian guards a different character. AOE that hits both the guarded and guardian bypasses guard. Some abilities specifically bypass guard. Amplitude = 0, lasts for duration turns
* Riposte: when attacked, attack back, does not trigger while stunned. Amplitude = how much riposte damage scales off of a character’s attack, lasts for duration of turns. Riposte damage can crit, using the character’s base crit chance.
* Restore: heal amplitude health at the start of a character’s turn, lasts for duration of turns.


Trinkets:
* Trinkets are equip-able items that grant various bonus to a Marionette and Ceci.
* Trinkets can grant a bonus to any stats, resistance, and status application chance
* Any Marionette and Ceci can equip any trinket, but only 1 of each, and only 2 trinkets at a time
* As the player’s team does not have an inventory, any trinket found must either be equipped immediately or discarded.
* Trinkets can be swapped around different team members, or temporarily taken off so a new one can be equipped, but once the selections have been confirmed, any trinket not equipped are discarded.

Economy:
* Throughout a run, the player earns 2 currency resources, Parts, and Scraps
* Parts and Scraps are dropped at the end of battle, the amount is slightly randomized.
* Some events can also reward small amounts of Parts and or Scraps.
* Elite battles drop more Parts and Scraps than normal battles.
* Parts are used to level up Ceci, or any Marionnette of the player’s choice, each level up costs progressively more Parts
* Scraps are used as currency for miscellaneous uses, such as buying items in shops, paying for events, etc
* Neither resources are carried to the next run


Skills:
* Skills are the main way characters interact with each other during combat
* Each class has a variety of skills, only 4 can be used in battle at a time
* Skills are unique to a class/monster, no skill is shared by multiple monsters or multiple classes
* Skills can have a variety of effects, from dealing damage, applying buffs and debuffs, to healing
* Some skills can only be used a set number of times per battle, these skills will have the restriction specified in its description.
* Skills have restriction on what position does a character need to be to use the skill, and what position can the skill hit
* Skills can be single target or hit up to 4 targets at once


Combat
* Combat is turn based, between the player’s team vs the computer controlled monster team
* Each team can have a maximum of 4 characters
* Characters on each team takes on 4 positions during combat, called ranks 1-4
* Ranks are mirrored between player and enemy teams, with rank 1 closest to middle of screen and rank 4 furthest
* Skills have limitations on which rank a character needs to be in to use, and which rank they can hit.
* At the start of a round, turn order of each character is determined based on speed
* Each character then takes turn performing action, when its a player character turn, the player can choose a skill to use, or the move action, allowing the character to swap rank with an adjacent ally, while enemies will pick a random skill available to them
* Some enemies can act more than once per round, each of this enemy’s turn order is still determined as if they're turns for separate characters.
* Once all characters have used up their turns for a round, the round ends and a new one start

Technical
Skills Database
* Uses ScriptableObjects to hold information about skills, each skill is 1 scriptable object
* Each skill has a unique ID, a display name string, a modifier list(refer to Skill Modifiers section)
* Each skill has info on which rank must the character be in to use, which rank can the skill hit
* Each skill has info on number of targets it can hit at once
* Each skill has info on whether it targets self, allies, or enemies
* Each skill can have a unique effects executed by script
* Skills use the programming strategy pattern to plug in the correct script to execute its unique effect
* The base skill constructs a SkillContext object and passes it to the execute(refer to Skill Context tab)


Skills Modifier
* Below is a list of possible modifiers a skill can have:
* Damage: percentage multiplier of a character’s attack stat
* Heal: percentage multiplier of a character’s attack stat
* Accuracy: additive to character’s base accuracy
* Critical: additive to character’s base critical chance
* Damage and Heal modifiers are mutually exclusive
* If a modifier is listed as 0(does not modify user’s base stat), UI does not display it


Character Database
* Uses ScriptableObjects to hold information about a character(marionnette and enemy)
* Each character has a unique ID, a display name string, a stat per level list
* Each index of stat per level list corresponds to 1 level a character can be
* Each index holds a stat block scriptable object that defines the specific numeric value of each stat a character has at that level.
* Game read stats at runtime using the character’s current level - 1(to account for 0 indexing) without modifying ScriptableObject
Skill Context
Overview
SkillContext represents all temporary, runtime data used during the execution of a skill.
It acts as a shared container that allows different systems (damage, status effects, AI interactions) to read and modify the same data during a skill’s resolution.
Key Principle:
SkillContext only contains per-execution data, not permanent character stats or static skill data. SkillContext can be modified throughout the game’s development to better fit with new design changes.
________________


Core Data
user (Character)
The character using the skill.
Reasoning: Needed for accessing stats, applying effects, and resolving interactions like crit chance or buffs.
skill (SkillData)
The skill being executed.
Reasoning: Provides static data such as scaling, targeting rules, and base effects.
targets (List)
All characters affected by the skill.
Reasoning: Supports multi-target and AoE skills.
primaryTarget (Character)
The main intended target.
Reasoning: Useful for single-target logic and effects that prioritize one target over others.
________________


Combat Calculation
baseAttackRoll (int)
Randomized attack value (80%–120% of attack stat).
Reasoning: Implements your defined attack variance system.
skillScaling (float)
Multiplier applied by the skill (e.g. 1.2 for 120%).
Reasoning: Allows skills to scale differently from base attack.
calculatedDamage (int)
Damage after calculations but before final application.
Reasoning: Shared value that multiple effects can modify.
damageMultiplier (float)
Additional modifier applied to damage. Default = 1.
Reasoning: Allows buffs, debuffs, and effects to stack cleanly without recalculating base damage.
________________


Critical System
isCritical (bool)
Whether the attack is a critical hit.
Reasoning: Determined once and reused by all effects.
critMultiplier (float)
Multiplier for critical damage (default 1.5).
Reasoning: Allows flexibility for buffs or special effects modifying crit damage.
________________


Hit Resolution
finalAccuracy (float)
Final accuracy after modifiers.
Reasoning: Needed to resolve hit/miss logic using Accuracy vs Dodge.
didHit (bool)
Whether the attack successfully hit.
Reasoning: Prevents effects from triggering on missed attacks.
________________


Special Interaction Flags
ignoresDefense (bool)
Attack bypasses defense.
Reasoning: Supports skills that ignore protection.
ignoresDodge (bool)
Attack cannot be dodged.
Reasoning: Used for guaranteed-hit mechanics.
guaranteedHit (bool)
Attack always hits regardless of accuracy.
Reasoning: Separate from ignore dodge for clarity and flexibility.
bypassGuard (bool)
Attack ignores guard/redirection mechanics.
Reasoning: Needed for specific counterplay skills.
________________


Multi-Hit Tracking
totalHits (int)
Total number of hits the skill performs.
Reasoning: Defines multi-hit skills.
currentHitIndex (int)
Current hit being processed.
Reasoning: Allows per-hit effects and status interactions (e.g. removing Flight stacks).
________________


Status System
pendingStatuses (List)
List of status effects to apply after execution.
Reasoning: Prevents immediate application conflicts and keeps resolution order consistent.
________________


System References
battleSystem (BattleSystem)
Reference to the battle system.
Reasoning: Allows effects to interact with turn order, targeting, or global rules.
rng (Random)
Random number generator.
Reasoning: Centralizes randomness for reproducibility and consistency.
________________


Flexible Extension
extra (Dictionary<string, object>)
Optional data for unique or complex mechanics.
Reasoning: Supports edge cases without modifying the core structure (e.g. boss-specific mechanics).
________________


Design Notes
* SkillContext is rebuilt for every skill use
* It is mutable, allowing effects and statuses to modify values during execution
* It works alongside the status system, which handles most conditional and ongoing mechanics
* Special mechanics (like Jailbird’s Flight) should be implemented as statuses, not hardcoded into SkillContext
________________


Summary
SkillContext acts as the execution layer of combat, enabling:
* consistent damage calculation
* flexible effect interactions
* clean separation between data and logic
* scalable handling of complex mechanics

Inputs and Interactions
Character Details
* Clicking on a marionette or Ceci opens up the marionette details UI on the correct page for that marionette/Ceci
* Clicking on the level up button increases the character’s level(if they have enough Parts) and updates the UI to display the new stats while also consuming the corresponding parts and updating the UI to reflect the parts consumption.
* Clicking on character tab shows the page of information for that character while highlighting the tab to show which tab is selected
Combat
* Clicking on a skills/move marks it as currently selected, and shows UI elements to prompt user to select target
* Select target UI is a small 2D sprite that appears at the feet of characters that are valid targets of the skill
* When hovered over applicable target, the sprite changes color to highlight which target is being selected
* Clicking on a valid target activates the skill on that target
* Hovering over characters display their stat in the stats display section of the UI
